Its or It's?

The single most common typo in English writing, explained in under a minute, then three tasks to prove you've got it.

The rule, actually

it's

A contraction. It only ever means it is or it has. The apostrophe stands in for missing letters — same job it does in "don't" or "can't."

its

A possessive pronoun, like his, hers, or theirs. None of those take an apostrophe either — so "its" doesn't, even though it feels like it should.

The test: say the sentence with "it is" swapped in. If it still makes sense, you want it's. If it sounds absurd, you want its. ("The dog wagged it is tail" — nope. So: "its tail.")
